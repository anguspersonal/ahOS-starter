Vocab guide — two axes

Every node carries two orthogonal tags: ahos:level (semantic role) and ahos:inBox (strict W3C schema/data). Filter along either axis using the toggle groups up top.

VocabProvidesUsed for
owl: the meta tools — Thing, Class, ObjectProperty, DatatypeProperty declaring what kind of building block something is
rdfs: the relation tools — subClassOf, label, domain, range linking blocks together
schema: (schema.org) domain concepts — Person, Organization, CreativeWork the things in the world
ahos: your coined concepts — Entity, KnowledgeFramework, Category the things specific to your ontology

Axis 1 — level (semantic role):

meta — the self-documenting view. The ontology talking about itself. Includes ahOntology (the document), ahos:level + ahos:inBox (the tagging properties), and the external standard vocab we rely on.
ontology — "what types of things exist and what do they show". The coined classes and properties — the schema of your vocab.
instance — "okay, here is the data". Concrete individuals typed against the classes.

Two-axis design for individuals (encoded in the graph):

Individuals carry two orthogonal pieces of metadata: rdf:type (what kind) and ahos:hasDomain (which sphere). This is itself documented structurally — see ahos:TypeAxis and ahos:DomainAxis nodes (visible when meta is on), connected to Category, Domain, and hasDomain via ahos:partOfAxis. Three queries fall out: "all X" (by type), "everything Y-flavoured" (by domain), "all Y-flavoured X" (intersection).

Axis 2 — box (strict W3C):

TBox — the schema. Anything declared a owl:Class, a owl:*Property, or a owl:Ontology.
ABox — the data. Every other named individual.

Today: meta + ontology → TBox; instance → ABox. The two axes correlate tightly. They're modelled separately so they CAN decorrelate — the Category-as-metaclass pattern means ahos:Projects is ABox today but functionally TBox-shaped. If you ever want to mark its dual role, you'd flip its inBox there.

And note: ahOntology is TBox (strict rule — it's a owl:Ontology) but plays a META role. Type ≠ role.
